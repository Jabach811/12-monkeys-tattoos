Protect the Art

Aftercare

Your tattoo is an open wound wearing a masterpiece. Here's how to keep it happy. Questions? Call the studio at (209) 839-1265.

Do

  • Remove the bandage any time after 2 hours.
  • Wash often with warm water and antibacterial soap — every 2–4 hours between bandaging.
  • Pat dry with a paper towel or let it air dry.
  • Apply a thin layer of healing ointment or mild moisturizer if it feels itchy, dry, or is peeling.
  • Reapply your own sterile bandage at your discretion.
  • See a doctor or urgent care if you notice signs of infection — redness, pain, swelling, unusual discharge, bumps, or a rash.

Don't

  • Don't scratch, pick, or rub any part of the healing tattoo.
  • Don't scrub off the top layer in the shower — let dead skin slough off naturally.
  • Don't expose it to direct sunlight.
  • Don't swim, surf, or soak — no pools, hot tubs, rivers, or ocean. Showers only.
  • Don't smother it with ointment or salve. Thin layers only.

What to Expect

The healing timeline

  1. Day 1

    Some fluid may ooze from the tattoo — blood, plasma, and extra ink. Skin will be red, sore, and slightly warm to the touch. Totally normal.

  2. Days 2–3

    The tattoo looks duller and cloudier as your skin starts healing. Scabs begin to form.

  3. Days 4–6

    Redness starts to fade and light scabbing appears. Leave the scabs alone — picking can cause scarring.

  4. Days 7–14

    Scabs harden and begin to flake off on their own. Still no picking.

  5. Days 15–30

    Most flakes and scabs are gone. The area may look dry and dull — keep it moisturized.

  6. Beyond

    The outer skin heals by week 2–3, but the deeper layers can take 3–4 months to fully settle. Average full heal: 2–6 weeks.
